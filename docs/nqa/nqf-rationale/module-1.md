# Level Assignment Rationale — Module 1

**Module:** Phishing Detection
**Assigned level:** Level 4
**Basis:** National qualifications framework level descriptors as currently published
**Review status:** Initial assignment (2026-11-12)

## Assignment statement

Module 1 is assessed at Level 4 of the national qualifications framework.
The assignment is conservative: the module's cognitive profile would also
support Level 5 assignment with the addition of two evaluation-level items.
Level 4 is assigned to allow institutional partners to credit the module at
the foundational level of a diploma program without level-dispute.

> **Note on descriptor wording:** The descriptor text below is a paraphrase of
> the framework's Level 4 standard structure. It is not a verbatim quotation.
> Verbatim descriptors are published in the framework standard document; the
> paraphrase is used here for readability. Reviewers comparing against the
> published standard should treat the wording as indicative, not as citation.
## Descriptor-by-descriptor analysis

### Level 4 descriptor: "Apply knowledge of a field of study or a broad field of learning"

**Evidence:**
- Outcomes O1 and O2 require application of authentication-quality and
  out-of-band-verification knowledge to operational scenarios.
- Items 2, 5, 7, 8, 11 collectively test application across five distinct
  operational contexts.

**Assessment:** This descriptor element is satisfied by the module.

### Level 4 descriptor: "Evaluate information for accuracy and validity"

**Evidence:**
- Outcome O3 requires analysis of inbound communications for impersonation
  indicators.
- Outcome O4 requires adjudication of competing priorities under
  operational constraint.
- Items 1, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12 test the learner's capacity to distinguish
  legitimate from fraudulent information and to weigh alternative courses
  of action.

**Assessment:** This descriptor element is exceeded. The module's cognitive
profile at these items is closer to the Level 5 descriptor
"apply critical analysis" than to Level 4 "evaluate."

### Level 4 descriptor: "Operate in a range of contexts, take responsibility for own work"

**Evidence:**
- Module requires decisions across technical, social-engineering,
  operational, forensic, and organizational contexts (six distinct contexts).
- Assessment conditions require independent judgment under mild time
  pressure; no collaborative answer possible.

**Assessment:** This descriptor element is satisfied.

### Level 4 descriptor: "Access, process, and manage information"

**Evidence:**
- Outcome O5 requires recognition of reporting-culture mechanisms and
  their effect on mean-time-to-detect.
- Module requires navigation of a study guide, practice bank, summative
  assessment, and reflective essays — a multi-modal information environment.

**Assessment:** This descriptor element is satisfied at minimum. Coverage
of O5 is thin (single item); descriptor satisfaction is marginal pending
addition of a second O5 item.

## Comparative level analysis

### Why not Level 3

Level 3 is characterized by application of knowledge and skills in a
**narrow range of contexts**. Module 1 spans six distinct operational contexts
and requires cross-context judgment. The scope exceeds Level 3 by design.

Additionally, Level 3 typically does not require the learner to adjudicate
between competing priorities; Module 1's Outcome 4 explicitly requires this.

### Why not Level 5

Level 5 requires **specialized theoretical knowledge** and the capacity to
**design, critique, or evaluate systems**. Module 1 does not teach theory;
it teaches first-responder judgment within existing control frameworks.

Level 5 content (security architecture, threat modeling, control selection,
framework critique) is concentrated in Tier B and Tier C modules:
- Tier B: Incident Response, Cloud Security, Database Security
- Tier C: ATT&CK mapping, Purple Teaming, Zero Trust architecture

Assigning Module 1 at Level 5 would create ambiguity between the module's
content and its assessment. Reviewers would correctly note that the module
tests judgment, not design.

## Cognitive-evidence basis

The assignment rests on item-level cognitive classification:

| Cognitive level | Item count | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| Recall | 0 | 0% |
| Comprehension | 0 | 0% |
| Application | 1 | 8.33% |
| Analysis | 10 | 83.33% |
| Evaluation | 1 | 8.33% |

The module is analysis-dominant. Analysis-dominant modules can be assigned
to Level 4 or Level 5 depending on the depth of analysis and the theoretical
content. Module 1 is assigned Level 4 because:
- Analysis is applied to practical scenarios, not theoretical constructs
- No item requires design or critique of a control architecture
- No item requires theoretical justification beyond established practice

## Credit calculation

**Notional hours:** 20
**Credit value:** 2 (design conversion: 1 credit = 10 notional hours, pending verification against current framework standard)

Breakdown:
- Guided study: 5 hours
- Practice items (formative): 5 hours
- Summative assessment + feedback review: 5 hours
- Reflective essays: 5 hours

If a partner institution uses a different credit-hour conversion, the
notional-hours basis is preserved and only the credit number changes. This
allows institutional flexibility without re-assignment of level.

## Review cycle

**Initial assignment:** 2026-11-12
**Scheduled review:** 2027-11-12
**Review triggers:**
- Three or more items replaced
- Any outcome modified
- National level descriptors revised
- Partner institution requests re-review

## Reviewer notes

The assignment is deliberately conservative. A reviewer who argues for
Level 5 assignment would not be wrong on cognitive grounds; the assignment
prioritizes institutional credit without dispute over level adjudication.

If a partner institution prefers Level 5, the assignment can be revised
within 30 minutes by adding two evaluation-level items. No content rewrite
required.
