# Learning Outcomes Specification — Module 1

**Module:** Phishing Detection
**Module ID:** 1
**Assigned level:** Level 4 (national qualifications framework descriptor)
**Notional learning hours:** 20
**Credit value:** 2 credits (design conversion: 1 credit = 10 notional hours, pending verification against current framework standard)
**Entry requirements:** None. Basic digital literacy assumed.

## Cognitive classification of outcomes

Outcomes are classified against a five-tier cognitive taxonomy
(recall → comprehension → application → analysis → evaluation). The module
targets outcomes distributed across application, analysis, and evaluation;
no outcome is pitched at comprehension or below.

## Declared outcomes

### Outcome 1 — Authentication-quality recognition
**Cognitive classification:** Recall + comprehension
**Statement:** The learner will recognize the operational distinction between
time-based one-time password authentication and phishing-resistant
authentication (public-key-bound credentials), and articulate why OTP alone
does not mitigate adversary-in-the-middle session establishment.

**Assessment:** Assessed by items 5, 7, and 11 of the assessment blueprint.

### Outcome 2 — Out-of-band verification
**Cognitive classification:** Application
**Statement:** The learner will apply pre-established out-of-band verification
protocols (known-number callback, internal directory extension) to payment
approval decisions in small-business operational contexts.

**Assessment:** Assessed by items 2 and 8 of the assessment blueprint.

### Outcome 3 — Impersonation discrimination
**Cognitive classification:** Analysis
**Statement:** The learner will analyze inbound communications and calls to
differentiate business email compromise and synthetic-media impersonation from
legitimate urgent communication, on the basis of observable indicators and
process deviations.

**Assessment:** Assessed by items 1, 6, 9, and 12 of the assessment blueprint.

### Outcome 4 — Priority adjudication under operational constraint
**Cognitive classification:** Evaluation
**Statement:** The learner will adjudicate between competing incident-response
priorities when credential-harvesting events coincide with workload constraints,
evidence-preservation obligations, and shift handover protocols, and will
defend the adjudication against plausible alternative courses of action.

**Assessment:** Assessed by items 3 and 4 of the assessment blueprint.

### Outcome 5 — Reporting-culture recognition
**Cognitive classification:** Comprehension + application
**Statement:** The learner will recognize the operational relationship between
blame-free reporting culture and mean-time-to-detect in small security teams,
and will identify the procedural mechanisms that sustain reporting velocity.

**Assessment:** Assessed by item 10 of the assessment blueprint. Thin coverage;
recommendation recorded in assessment blueprint for expansion.

## Comparative level analysis

**Why not Level 3:** Level 3 assessments are restricted to a narrow range of
contexts. Module 1 tests judgment across technical, social-engineering,
operational, forensic, and organizational contexts. That range exceeds Level 3.

**Why not Level 5:** Level 5 requires demonstration of specialized theoretical
knowledge and the capacity to design or critique systems. Module 1 tests
first-responder judgment, not architectural design. Level 5 content is
concentrated in Tier B and Tier C modules.

## Credit calculation

**Notional hours:**

- Guided study (module guide + resources): 5 hours
- Practice item bank (unlimited retakes): 5 hours
- Summative assessment + feedback review: 5 hours
- Reflective essays (research, drafting, revision): 5 hours

**Total:** 20 notional hours = 2 credits at national credit conversion.
